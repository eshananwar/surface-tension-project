01 / THE BIG IDEA

Water has a
hidden strength.

Have you ever seen a tiny insect stand on water? Or watched a carefully placed paper clip float? These surprising moments happen because of surface tension.

It makes the surface of a liquid behave somewhat like a thin, flexible skin. It is not an actual covering — it is a result of the attraction between water molecules.

✦

Our central question
How can the surface of ordinary water support things we might expect to sink?

LET'S FIND OUT  ↘
02 / UNDERSTAND THE SCIENCE

Small molecules.
Big behaviour.

Water molecules attract one another. At the surface, this attraction creates a special effect we call surface tension.

THE MOLECULAR VIEW

Meet the
water molecules.

Inside the water, molecules are pulled in every direction. At the surface, the molecules experience a net pull inward. This helps the surface resist being stretched.

03 / LIVE DEMONSTRATIONS

Three experiments.
One amazing principle.

Watch how surface tension reveals itself in simple, surprising ways.

⌁
FLOATING ON THE SURFACE
EXPERIMENT 0101 / 03

The floating
paper clip.

A paper clip is gently placed on the water using a small piece of tissue. When the tissue becomes wet and sinks, the carefully placed clip can remain floating.

WHAT TO NOTICEThe clip rests on the surface without breaking through it.

A CHANGE IN SURFACE TENSION
EXPERIMENT 0202 / 03

The dancing
pepper.

Pepper sprinkled on water spreads across the surface. When a little dish soap touches the water, the pepper quickly moves away from that spot.

WHAT TO NOTICEThe pepper moves towards the edges after soap touches the water.
